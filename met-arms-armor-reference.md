## Overview

The Met Museum's Arms and Armor department (Department ID: 4) holds one of the most extensive collections of medieval and early modern arms and armor in the world. A keyword search for "medieval armor sword" within this department, filtered to objects with images, returned **386 object IDs**.

This document compiles detailed metadata for **10 representative pieces** selected from those search results, covering helmets, full armors, horse armor, shields, gauntlets, and arm defenses. Together they give a cross-section of European and Japanese arms-making traditions, materials, and techniques.

## Research Notes

### Search Methodology
- Queried the Met Museum API for objects in the Arms and Armor department (ID: 4) matching "medieval armor sword" with the `hasImages` flag set to `true`.
- Result: 386 matching object IDs.
- Retrieved detailed records (metadata + images) for 10 representative object IDs selected from the result set.

### Materials Observed Across the Set
- **Metals:** Steel, iron, copper alloy (brass/latten), silver, gold, shakudō (copper-gold alloy)
- **Decorative techniques:** Cloisonné enamel, etching/gilding implied by gold/silver overlays, lacquer (Japanese piece)
- **Organic materials:** Leather, textile, silk, horsehair, ivory
- **Construction:** Plate armor, mail (composed armor includes a mail shirt), laminated/lacquered Japanese construction

### Notable Sub-Collections Represented
- **Bashford Dean Memorial Collection** (multiple pieces): A key early 20th-century bequest that formed a core of the Met's arms and armor holdings.
- **Fletcher Fund** purchases (1938, 1921): Important 20th-century acquisitions including a complete garniture and horse armor.
- **Rogers Fund** (1904, 1933): Funded high-profile acquisitions including the Ferdinand I armor and a pair of gauntlets.

### Geographic and Chronological Spread
- **Europe:** German (Nuremberg — Kunz Lochner), Italian (Collalto horse armor, Völs-Colonna garniture), Spanish (Julian Arrechia composed armor)
- **Japan:** Gusoku by Bamen Tomotsugu (18th-century Echizen province)
- **Date range of manufacture:** Roughly 16th–18th centuries, with some 19th–early 20th-century restoration/composition.
